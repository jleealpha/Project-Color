Color Vision Screening Test

Before You Begin

What to Expect

The color vision screening test consists of two parts, designed to assess your color vision comprehensively:

  • Arrangement-Style Plates (Part 1): You'll view a series of colored dot patterns and identify the numbers or shapes you see. This test is quick and painless.
  • Anomaloscope Test (Part 2): You'll adjust colors to match a reference color, similar to a clinical color-matching test. This allows us to measure the characteristics of your color vision more precisely.

Expected duration: 5–10 minutes total. Take your time—there's no time limit.

Display Calibration

For accurate results, your display should be in a good state for color perception. Please prepare your monitor:

  • Brightness: Set your monitor to a comfortable brightness—not too dim and not too bright. A good rule of thumb is that you should be able to clearly distinguish light grays from white.
  • Avoid glare: Position yourself so glare from windows or bright lights doesn't wash out the colors on your screen.
  • Disable blue-light filters: If your monitor or device has a blue-light filter (e.g., Night Light on Windows), consider disabling it for the duration of the test. These filters can distort color perception.
  • Ambient lighting: A dimly lit room is ideal. Very bright ambient light can interfere with color perception on the screen.
  • Color profile: This test assumes a standard sRGB display. If you're using a specialized color-managed monitor (e.g., for photo editing), results may vary.

Important Notes

  • No medical diagnosis: This test is a screening tool and is not a substitute for a clinical eye exam by an optometrist or ophthalmologist.
  • Privacy: Your results are computed in your browser and not stored on our servers. You'll be able to download or print your result at the end.
  • One test per session: You can take the test as many times as you like, but each attempt is independent.
